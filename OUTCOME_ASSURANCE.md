# Purpose and Outcome Assurance

CLSES 4.0 treats the reason for a software system as an empirical, revisable claim rather than a one-time statement of intent.

The system definition identifies the demonstrated need, beneficiaries, purpose, operating conditions, and measurable outcome hypotheses. Each hypothesis defines a metric, unit, comparison operator, target, minimum number of sustained observation periods, and assessment horizon.

The base production profile requires evidence for demonstrated need, stakeholder representation, alternatives, intervention logic, measurement design, benefit versus risk, economic value, ethical impact, realized benefit, and continuing justification. These obligations cannot be removed by an overlay.

An outcome evaluation is conformant only when production-grounded evidence covers every hypothesis; the target holds for at least the declared number of non-overlapping periods; the evaluation states a counterfactual and attribution method; realized value is not less than lifecycle cost; assumptions remain evidenced; and every declared harm is absent or mitigated.

A separate outcome authority must bind all outcome-control decisions and evaluations to one of the permitted actions. A positive adequacy verdict permits only `continue` or `expand`. Corrective, restrictive, suspension, and retirement actions remain valid governance responses, but they do not assert that purpose is currently achieved and sustained.

The continuation decision has a mandatory review date bounded by the underlying evidence. Closure binds the exact continuation action. Evidence expiry, an invalid assumption, an unmet period, deployment drift, a material change, or an unmitigated harm makes the positive verdict fail closed.
